### Social

From being present where people are to hosting and deepening: small reps in the places your week already holds, each your own act. What anyone else says or does is never the measure.

#### Stage 1 · Presence

Be where people are and be seen: eye contact, a greeting by name, attention on them rather than on yourself.

- Not progress: Rehearsing alone, watching others talk, or scrolling in the room: they fill a day and move nothing.
- How it moves: moves by the rule

#### Stage 6 · Host, lead, deepen

- Not progress: Big events planned and never held; depth pressed on someone who has not offered it.
- How it moves: moves by the rule
- **Introduce two people to each other** (moves the stage · where: a group, a recurring place · proposed). Attention on: Their names, and the one thing they share. Drop: Leaving each to find the other. Done when you have introduced them, however it goes.

- **Take a small recurring role** (moves the stage · where: a recurring place, a group · proposed). Attention on: Who you can help while you are there. Drop: Arriving just in time and leaving first. Done each time you do the role.
- **Go one level deeper** (moves the stage · where: one to one · proposed). Attention on: One question you would like to be asked yourself. Drop: Keeping it to the weather because it is safe. Done when you have asked, however they answer.

#### How a stage is reached

Design judgment, not a research finding. A stage is yours once you have done six of its reps within six weeks, across at least three different reps and two kinds of setting. Two No or Not now answers in a row bring the smallest version of the same stage. After four quiet weeks, reps from one stage below come back for re-entry, while the stage you reached stands; nothing ever resets to the start, and a missed day changes nothing. Solo and remote reps fill days with nobody around and never move a stage. One people rep a day across the whole app.

- Source: Craske, Treanor, Conway, Zbozinek and Vervliet (2014). Exposure works through new learning, helped by variety across settings, people and reps; the benefit of variety over a fixed order is supported only in part. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Kircanski and colleagues; Knowles, Jessup and Olatunji (2012). Exposure in a random or variable order did as well as a graded hierarchy, in small analogue samples. Evidence: thin.
- Source: Rowe and Craske (1998). Exposure spread across a week held at follow-up where the same exposure massed into one day saw fear return. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Sandstrom, Boothby and Cooney (2022). One short conversation a day for a week made people less pessimistic about rejection, lasting at least a week. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts and Wardle (2010). Missing one opportunity did not materially affect how a habit formed; the median time to automaticity was sixty-six days. Evidence: moderate.

Kinds of setting:

- a recurring place: A place your week already holds: work, church, daycare, the gym. The home of the path.
- an errand: A shop, a queue, a counter: variety, never the home.
- a group: Three or more people talking together.
- one to one: Just the two of you.
- a call or a message: A call or a message: fills a day with nobody around, and moves no stage.
- on your own: On your own: a warm-up that moves no stage.

#### Counted, and never counted

Reps done, by rep and by setting, with their dates. A rep is done when you did your part, however the other person answered.

Never counted, stored or asked: yeses and noes; replies, or whether anyone wrote back; matches, likes and profile views; numbers exchanged; dates obtained, and second dates; how many people; any rating of a person, a conversation, a date or a rejection; streaks of approaches; time taken to reach a stage or a milestone; a second ask after a no.

#### Left out, and why

- Power posing: Its effects on hormones and behaviour did not replicate; what remains is small and comes from not slumping (a disputed card).
- Positive self-statements: Repeating them left people with low self-esteem feeling worse, and the finding stands unreplicated (a disputed card).
- Watching instead of doing: Modelling changes knowledge more than behaviour, and people who only observed did not change: solo rehearsal is a warm-up and never moves a stage.
- Waiting to feel calm: How far fear falls during practice does not predict the result, so no stage waits for you to feel ready.
- Scripts and memorised openers: Plain, specific sentences about the moment or the other person; a line recited from memory is not a rep here.

#### Stage 5 · Deciding

Decide rather than slide: your values and non-negotiables written down before they are tested, a decide-don’t-slide note before each step, a monthly private check on your own safety, conduct and doubts, and education before any commitment.

- Not progress: Letting a step happen because it is easier than choosing it.
- How it moves: moves only when you declare it
- **Your values and non-negotiables, written down.** A short private note, typed by you, of what you value in a partner and the few things you will not accept, written before they are tested. Only you read it. Source: Jonason, Garcia, Webster, Li and Fisher (2015). People weigh dealbreakers more heavily than the qualities they want; knowing yours in advance is supported. Evidence: moderate.
- **Decide, don’t slide.** Before each step (becoming exclusive, meeting each other’s families, moving in, an engagement), a private note: what you are choosing, and why now. Source: Owen, Rhoades and Stanley (2013). People who reported deciding their relationship steps thoughtfully reported more dedication and satisfaction than those who slid into them. Evidence: moderate.
- **A monthly private check.** Once a month, three private questions about your own safety, your own conduct, and any doubt you keep setting aside. The app shows fixed help itself whenever your answers call for it, and no model ever writes or softens it. The questions: In the last month, has anyone you are dating made you afraid, or tried to control where you go, whom you see or what you spend? #### Stage 6 · Building

The small acts that build a relationship: meet good news with interest, thank specifically, do something new together, reappraise a disagreement.

- Not progress: Keeping a tally of who did more; a compatibility quiz.
- How it moves: moves only when you declare it
- **Meet their good news with real interest** (moves no stage · where: one to one · proposed). Attention on: Their face when they tell it. Drop: Topping it with news of your own. Done when you did it.
- **Thank your partner for one specific thing** (moves no stage · where: one to one · proposed). Attention on: The thing they did, and what it saved or gave you. Drop: A general thanks for everything. Done when you have said it.
- **Do one new thing together** (moves no stage · where: one to one · proposed). Attention on: Something neither of you would plan alone. Drop: The same evening as always. Done when you did it together.
- **Reappraise a disagreement** (moves no stage · where: on your own · proposed). Attention on: How a fair friend of you both would tell it. Drop: Replaying who was right. Done when you have written it.

#### Stage 7 · Keeping

The same acts over years, talking openly about what you each want, and the monthly check still yours.

- Not progress: Counting on love to keep itself.
- How it moves: moves only when you declare it
- **Talk openly about what you each want** (moves no stage · where: one to one · proposed). Attention on: What you each like, said kindly. Drop: Waiting for it to come up on its own. Done when you have talked.

#### How a stage is reached

The first three stages move by the same rule as Social: six reps within six weeks, across three different reps and two kinds of setting. Saying you have a date moves you to Dating at once. From Dating onwards a stage moves only when you declare it, in one tap, dated and reversible; milestones are dated notes, never counts. Online and remote reps move no stage.

- Source: Craske, Treanor, Conway, Zbozinek and Vervliet (2014). Exposure works through new learning, helped by variety across settings, people and reps; the benefit of variety over a fixed order is supported only in part. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Kircanski and colleagues; Knowles, Jessup and Olatunji (2012). Exposure in a random or variable order did as well as a graded hierarchy, in small analogue samples. Evidence: thin.
- Source: Rowe and Craske (1998). Exposure spread across a week held at follow-up where the same exposure massed into one day saw fear return. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Sandstrom, Boothby and Cooney (2022). One short conversation a day for a week made people less pessimistic about rejection, lasting at least a week. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts and Wardle (2010). Missing one opportunity did not materially affect how a habit formed; the median time to automaticity was sixty-six days. Evidence: moderate.

Kinds of setting:

- a recurring place: A place your week already holds: work, church, daycare, the gym. The home of the path.
- an errand: A shop, a queue, a counter: variety, never the home.
- a group: Three or more people talking together.
- one to one: Just the two of you.
- a call or a message: A call or a message: fills a day with nobody around, and moves no stage.
- on your own: On your own: a warm-up that moves no stage.

#### Channel: Online

An optional, bounded channel, at your word on 2026-09-23. Off until you turn it on, and one switch turns it fully off again: then none of its reps is offered and none is kept. It is never required for any stage and never the route a stage is met by: its reps are solo or remote, and those move no stage. It is bounded: a browse is fifteen minutes and stops, no more than two of its reps are offered in a week, and it points at meeting in person soon. Nothing about how anyone responds is kept or shown: the never-counted list applies to it in full.

#### Counted, and never counted

Reps done, by rep and by setting, with their dates, and the stages and milestones you declare. A rep is done when you did your part, whatever the answer.

Never counted, stored or asked: yeses and noes; replies, or whether anyone wrote back; matches, likes and profile views; numbers exchanged; dates obtained, and second dates; how many people; any rating of a person, a conversation, a date or a rejection; streaks of approaches; time taken to reach a stage or a milestone; a second ask after a no.

#### Left out, and why

- Approaching strangers in the street: No study maps where such approaches are welcome, and unwanted attention from strangers is common and harmful.
- Reading signals to decide who is interested: Flirting is detected poorly, and people afraid of rejection think their own overtures say more than they do: say it plainly instead.
- Lines, tactics and games: Cute or flippant lines are rated worst, a plain or third-party introduction best, and endorsing pickup tactics tracks hostile sexism.
- A type, a checklist or a compatibility score: Ideals barely predict whom people come to desire, and what predicts a good relationship is how it works, not a match of traits.
- Asking again after a no: Persistence after a no is the core of harassment; one ask, and anything other than a yes is final.
- Someone you supervise, or who supervises you: Harm around workplace romance concentrates where one partner has power over the other (a framework, not a trial).
- Matching algorithms, love languages and divorce prediction: None holds up on the evidence (disputed cards); the path never uses them.
- Labelling a partner’s attachment style, or opposites attract: Verdicts on another person are never made here, and actual similarity barely predicts satisfaction either way.

#### For a parent

For a parent, in general terms. One study found a mother’s dating after divorce had little direct effect on her children’s adjustment; several changes in who lives in a child’s home go with more behaviour problems, partly because of what came before, and a systematic review found that evidence mixed. When and how to introduce a partner to a child has no tested answer: the guidance is expert opinion. The path never schedules an introduction; that stays yours.

## The research the layer rests on

Read before wiring: what each body of work contributes as entries, as tags, and as the wording of the two evening chips.

### Behavioural activation

- Source: Martell, Addis and Jacobson (2001). Depression in Context: act from the outside in; schedule by value, not by mood; avoidance is the target. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Lejuez, Hopko and Hopko (2001). Brief behavioural activation treatment: activity monitoring, then graded scheduling of valued activities. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Dimidjian and colleagues (2006). Randomised trial: behavioural activation matched medication and beat cognitive therapy for more severe depression. Evidence: strong.
- Source: Ekers and colleagues (2014). Meta-analysis of 26 trials: behavioural activation works, delivered simply. Evidence: strong.

What it contributes:

- The move comes before the mood: the app offers a move at the reading, never waits for the wish to do it.
- Pleasure and mastery as learned tags: the two kinds of activity behavioural activation schedules, with connection added from self-determination theory.
- Graded task assignment: the smaller version on a study night, and the low-effort tag as the top filter.
- Trigger, response, avoidance pattern to alternative coping: the entry "Notice it and act anyway".
- Activity monitoring: the record itself, and the counts under Follow-through.

The two chips: "Nothing landed today" is behavioural activation’s "no mastery or pleasure today" in plain words. The answer shows how many times before and what the following days looked like, from the record, never a verdict.

### Acceptance and commitment therapy, values

- Source: Hayes, Strosahl and Wilson (2012). Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: values, committed action, willingness, defusion. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: A-Tjak and colleagues (2015). Meta-analysis of 39 randomised trials: ACT beat controls with g about 0.4. Evidence: strong.
- Source: Wilson and Murrell (2004). Values work: chosen directions, never goals to finish, never scored. Evidence: moderate.

What it contributes:

- Your direction sentence: one chosen line, shown back unchanged, never counted or graded. No values questionnaire, no "what matters to me" list.
- Committed action: the protected step above the move, which the reading cannot displace.
- Willingness and defusion: "Notice it and act anyway", and the smaller version instead of the skip.

The two chips: "Hard to see the point today" is answered with your direction line and the record, never argued with. The app does not reason with the feeling; it shows what you chose and what you did.

### Self-determination theory

- Source: Ryan and Deci (2000). Autonomy, competence and relatedness as the three needs that carry motivation and wellbeing. Evidence: strong.
- Source: Reis, Sheldon, Gable, Roscoe and Ryan (2000). Daily diaries: satisfying the three needs in a day predicted that day’s wellbeing. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Sheldon, Elliot, Kim and Kasser (2001). Across cultures, autonomy, competence and relatedness ranked as what made satisfying events satisfying. Evidence: moderate.

What it contributes:

- The three reward tags map onto the three needs: pleasure alongside competence (mastery) and relatedness (connection).
- Autonomy: you always decide; faith is offered never pushed; a chip is a statement, never a command.
- Competence without evaluation: counts, never grades, streaks or readiness scores.

The two chips: A chip’s answer names what was done and who was around, from the record, because competence and relatedness are what a day is made of; it never tells you what to feel.

### Meaning in life

- Source: Steger, Frazier, Oishi and Kaler (2006). The Meaning in Life Questionnaire: presence of meaning and search for it are separate things. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: King, Hicks, Krull and Del Gaiso (2006). Six studies: positive affect raises the felt meaning of life, day by day. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Heintzelman and King (2014). Review: most people rate their lives as meaningful; routine and coherence carry much of it. Evidence: moderate.
- Source: Martela and Steger (2016). Three facets: coherence, purpose, significance. Evidence: moderate.

What it contributes:

- Coherence: what the following days looked like, shown from the record when a chip is tapped.
- Purpose: the direction line and the protected step.
- Significance: the dated counts under Becoming.
- Positive affect raises felt meaning, so the calm, factual reply is the therapeutic one; nothing here diagnoses, and the crisis screen stays dropped.

The two chips: "Hard to see the point today" is a report about the day, never a verdict on a life. The reply is the record: the direction line, and the counts of what was done.

## The extension prompt, as the app will write it

```
You are extending the catalogue of moves for Life Mirror, a phone app for one person. Read the record below and write {n} new entries.

THE RECORD
Situations that run short of candidates: {situations: block, lowest reading, band, candidates left}
Moves offered and never done: {moves: name, offered, done}
Families the filters make unreachable: {families: name, the filter that blocks them}
Moves that held up, and where: {moves: name, situation, tier}

THE RULES
No entry says the same or the opposite thing as an existing one; the conflict and replaces fields are enforced in code.
Every entry carries a source (who, what, year) and its strength: strong, moderate, thin or practice.
Learned tags only from: outdoors, with people, short, low effort; pleasure, mastery, connection; intensity. Filter tags: cost to assign, starting effort, needs, effect window.
One line sized to one sitting; minutes and effort to start; which readings it should move, in which direction, over which window.
A reading, never a verdict: none of the six words the build bans; no streaks, no grades.
Nothing personal, no names, and no private item named or implied.

THE OUTPUT
JSON entries matching the catalogue schema, ready to paste into the catalogue for reading and veto.
```
